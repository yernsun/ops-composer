# Contributor License Agreement readiness policy

[简体中文](CLA_POLICY.zh-CN.md) | English

> **Status: not active.** This document is a governance policy, not a CLA and not an agreement that
> can presently be signed. External copyrightable contributions must not be merged until the
> activation requirements below are complete.

## Purpose

OpsComposer is released under `AGPL-3.0-only`. The project may later offer a separate commercial
license for code it has the right to license. A CLA is intended to give the project steward a
clear, auditable permission chain while contributors keep ownership of their contributions.

The CLA must not be described as revoking open-source rights. AGPL licenses already granted are
irrevocable while their conditions are met. A future commercial license would be an additional
choice and could cover only rights controlled by its licensor.

## Activation requirements

Before accepting any CLA, the project must publish and obtain qualified legal review of:

1. the exact legal name, address, and authority of the party receiving the license;
2. individual and, where needed, corporate CLA forms with version identifiers;
3. governing law, venue or dispute process, notice details, and an effective date;
4. an electronic-signature or other acceptance process that records identity, authority, CLA
   version, contribution identity, timestamp, and evidence of assent;
5. a privacy notice and access/retention/deletion controls for CLA records; and
6. a public allowlist or automated check that blocks merging until acceptance is verified.

The final CLA should be localized only with a clause identifying which language controls. Do not
activate an informal translation as a separate contract by accident.

## Minimum substantive terms for counsel to review

The final CLA should, at minimum:

- define the project, contribution, submission channels, contributor, and receiving party;
- let the contributor retain ownership;
- grant a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, irrevocable copyright license to
  reproduce, modify, prepare derivatives, publicly display/perform, distribute, sublicense, and
  relicense the contribution and derivatives under open-source and proprietary terms;
- grant an appropriately scoped patent license, normally with a defensive-termination provision;
- confirm the contributor has authority, including employer permission where applicable;
- require disclosure of third-party code, licenses, patents, and other known restrictions;
- state that contributions are provided without a support obligation and with legally appropriate
  warranty disclaimers; and
- exclude submissions clearly marked `Not a Contribution` and provide a correction/contact process.

Whether moral-rights language, representations, governing law, an entity definition, or a separate
corporate CLA is enforceable or appropriate depends on jurisdiction and must be decided by counsel.
A copyright assignment is not the current policy; changing to assignment would require an explicit
project decision and fresh legal review.

## Limits of the CLA

A CLA does not:

- change the license of third-party components or let the project sell rights it does not own;
- automatically cover an employer, an earlier anonymous commit, or material copied without authority;
- promise that a contribution will be merged or that a commercial license will be offered; or
- create support, employment, payment, partnership, or fiduciary obligations.

Before commercial dual licensing, conduct a commit-by-commit rights audit, resolve all pre-CLA and
third-party material, and define which deliverable excludes or isolates incompatible copyleft
components. See [docs/legal/dependency-compliance.md](docs/legal/dependency-compliance.md).
